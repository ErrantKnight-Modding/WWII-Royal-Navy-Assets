Historical Overview:
HMS Nelson was the lead ship of her class of two dreadnought battleships built for the Royal Navy under the strict 35,000-ton limitations imposed by the 1922 Washington Naval Treaty. To economize on armored citadel length and weight, the design pioneered the radical concentration of the entire main battery into three triple 16-inch turrets mounted forward of the bridge tower ("Queen Anne's Mansion"), earning the pair the nicknames "Nelsol" and "Rodnol" (after Admiralty auxiliary fleet tankers) and the "Cherry Tree class" (cut down by the Washington Treaty).

During World War II, HMS Nelson played a distinguished role across multiple naval theaters. Serving as flagship of Force H based at Gibraltar under Vice-Admiral Sir James Somerville, Nelson escorted critical relief convoys through the Mediterranean to besieged Malta, participating in Operation Substance and Operation Halberd. During Halberd in September 1941, she was struck forward by an Italian aerial torpedo while protecting convoy transports, surviving with severe flooding thanks to her formidable multi-compartment torpedo protection system.

Following repairs, Nelson supported Operation Torch (the Allied invasion of French North Africa) in November 1942. In July and September 1943, her 16-inch guns provided heavy bombardment during Operation Husky (the invasion of Sicily) and Operation Avalanche (Salerno). On 29 September 1943, General Dwight D. Eisenhower and Italian Marshal Pietro Badoglio signed the instrument of the Long Armistice of Cassibile aboard HMS Nelson in Malta harbor.

In June 1944, Nelson participated in Operation Overlord (the Normandy Landings), delivering devastating heavy gunfire support against German coastal defenses and Panzer concentrations inland before suffering mine damage. Deployed to the Indian Ocean in 1945, Nelson accepted the surrender of Japanese forces at Penang and Singapore before returning to Britain to serve as a training ship and flagship of the Home Fleet until her decommissioning in 1948.

Mission Editor Notes & HMS Rodney Stand-In:
- Stand-In for HMS Rodney (29): HMS Nelson can serve as operational stand-in for her sister ship HMS Rodney. Nelson and Rodney were the sole members of the Nelson class, sharing identical hull dimensions, displacement, armor protection, the radical all-forward concentration of three triple 16-inch BL Mark I turrets ('A', 'B', 'X'), and the distinctive "Queen Anne's Mansion" bridge tower.
- Anti-Aircraft Configuration (UP Rocket Removal): Early in the war (1940-1941), both ships were equipped with experimental 7-inch Unrotated Projectile (UP) anti-aircraft rocket launchers (four mounts on Nelson, one on Rodney). Following operational trials that proved the system hazardous and ineffective, the UP launchers were removed from both vessels during refits in late 1941 / early 1942 in favor of additional 2-pounder octuple Pom-Poms and 20mm Oerlikons. The absence of UP rocket launchers on this model accurately represents the class in its post-1942 mid-to-late war combat fit.
- Submerged Torpedo Tubes: Both battleships were originally built with two submerged 24.5-inch torpedo tubes in the underwater bow citadel below the waterline. Following mine damage in December 1939, Nelson's submerged torpedo room was decommissioned. While Rodney famously retained hers to launch submerged torpedoes against the German battleship Bismarck on 27 May 1941 (the only known instance in naval history of a battleship torpedoing another), these submerged underwater tubes were invisible externally and were decommissioned in later refits.
- Major Operations for Rodney Scenarios: Deploying this asset as HMS Rodney enables historical recreation of her landmark operations, including the final action against the Bismarck (May 1941), Force H Malta convoys and Operation Pedestal (August 1942), Operation Torch (November 1942), Operation Avalanche at Salerno (September 1943), heavy naval gunfire support (NGS) off Sword and Juno beaches during Operation Overlord (Normandy, June 1944), and Arctic convoy escort (1944).
