HMS Foxhound (H69) was an F-class fleet destroyer built for the Royal Navy under the 1932 Naval Programme. Laid down at John Brown & Company, Clydebank on 21 August 1933, launched on 12 October 1934, and commissioned on 6 June 1935, Foxhound rendered distinguished service throughout the Second World War.

Specifications:
- Displacement: 1,405 long tons (standard), 1,950 long tons (full load)
- Length: 329 ft (100.3 m) oa, 315 ft (96.0 m) pp
- Beam: 33 ft 3 in (10.13 m)
- Draught: 12 ft 6 in (3.81 m)
- Installed Power: 36,000 shp (27,000 kW), 3 Admiralty 3-drum boilers
- Propulsion: 2 shafts, Parsons geared steam turbines
- Speed: 36 knots (67 km/h; 41 mph)
- Complement: 145 officers and ratings

Armament (as fitted, 1940-1941):
- 4 x single 4.7-inch (120 mm) QF Mark IX guns on CP XXII mountings (Turrets A, B, X, Y)
- 2 x quadruple Vickers .50-inch (12.7 mm) Mark III anti-aircraft heavy machine guns
- 2 x quadruple 21-inch (533 mm) torpedo tube mountings
- 1 x depth charge rack and 2 x throwers (20 depth charges)

Sensors & Fire Control:
- 3-meter coincidence optical rangefinder director atop bridge
- Type 286 / Type 271 surface search radar
- Type 124 ASDIC (sonar)

Operational History:
- Assigned to the 8th Destroyer Flotilla, Home Fleet.
- On 14 September 1939, Foxhound assisted in the sinking of German submarine U-39 after it attempted an attack on the aircraft carrier HMS Ark Royal.
- During the Norwegian Campaign in April 1940, Foxhound participated in the Second Battle of Narvik under the battleship HMS Warspite, engaging German destroyers in Ofotfjord.
- Transferred to Force H based at Gibraltar in mid-1940, escorting capital ships during Operation Catapult, Operation Collar, and multiple Malta relief convoys.
- In 1944, Foxhound was transferred to the Royal Canadian Navy and renamed HMCS Qu'Appelle (H69), escorting Allied invasion fleets during Operation Overlord in the English Channel and hunting U-boats in the Bay of Biscay.
