Operational History:
- Interwar Fleet Carrier: Converted from a "Large Light Cruiser" under the Washington Naval Treaty, Glorious served extensively with the Mediterranean Fleet throughout the 1930s, pioneering carrier aviation tactics, night deck landings, and synchronized multi-squadron strikes.
- Outbreak of War & Indian Ocean: In October 1939, Glorious joined Force J in the Indian Ocean and Red Sea to hunt the German pocket battleship Admiral Graf Spee.
- Norwegian Campaign (April-June 1940): In April 1940, Glorious was recalled to the Home Fleet for Operation Alphabet. Her Sea Gladiators and Swordfish provided close air support and intercepted Luftwaffe bombers assaulting Allied ground forces at Narvik and Åndalsnes.
- Historic RAF Fighter Landings (21 May & 7 June 1940): In a daring aviation milestone, RAF No. 263 Squadron (Gloster Gladiators) and No. 46 Squadron (Hawker Hurricanes) flew off Glorious to northern Norwegian airstrips, and on 7 June successfully landed their Hurricanes back aboard Glorious at sea without tailhooks or arresting gear—the first time high-performance land monoplane fighters had ever successfully recovered onto an aircraft carrier.
- Sinking (8 June 1940): While returning to Scapa Flow escorted only by the fleet destroyers HMS Acasta and HMS Ardent, Glorious was surprised in the Norwegian Sea by the German battleships Scharnhorst and Gneisenau. Caught without reconnaissance patrols aloft, she was hit at extreme range (24,000 m) by 28 cm shells from Scharnhorst that pierced her flight deck into the upper hangar, setting fire to fueled aircraft. Despite heroic torpedo attacks by Acasta and Ardent that severely damaged Scharnhorst, Glorious sank with the loss of over 1,200 men.

Mission Editor Notes & HMS Furious Stand-In:
- Stand-In for HMS Furious (47): With flight-deck catapults omitted in this configuration, HMS Glorious can serve as an operational stand-in for her sister ship HMS Furious. Unlike Glorious, Furious operated without flight deck catapults throughout World War II, utilizing unobstructed full-length deck runs for takeoff and transverse arrester wires for recoveries. Furious served as a premier frontline fleet carrier throughout 1941–1944, actively flying operations during the Malta Club Runs, Operation Bellows (flying off 38 Spitfires into besieged Malta on 11 August 1942 as part of Operation Pedestal), Operation Torch (North Africa 1942), and the Home Fleet carrier air strikes against the German battleship Tirpitz in northern Norway (Operation Tungsten 1944).
